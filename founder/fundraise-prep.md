# MeterProof fundraise prep

Assumption: the company is South African, sells only in South African metros, and raises in rand.
Assumption: the founder is full-time on MeterProof. If not, the Team score drops to 1.
Assumption: R18 to the US dollar for comparing with dollar-denominated data (estimate).

From `founder/facts.md`: pre-launch with no users or revenue, raising R1m pre-seed, solo founder, deck sent ahead.

## 1. Readiness assessment

| Dimension | Score | What investors want to see | The gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traction | 1 | Pre-seed: a team and an idea, plus any sign that people want it | Zero users, zero payments. Not even a waitlist. |
| Team | 2 | Full-time, technical, domain experience | Technical and full-time (assumed), but solo, with no stated utility, billing or legal background. Investors will ask who handles growth and support. |
| Market | 2 | A path to a large outcome | The pain is real and sourced. But the 3-year SOM in the deck is R21.8m a year (about $1.2m), which is a good business and too small for most venture funds. |
| Product | 4 | Working MVP, user feedback, retention | Fully built, 7 metros, payments live in sandbox. No user feedback or retention data yet. |
| Unit economics | 2 | CAC, LTV, gross margin, even rough | Margins estimated at 81% to 92%. CAC unknown, LTV assumes churn nobody has measured. |
| Story | 4 | 30 seconds, and someone cares | "1 in 8 eThekwini water readings are estimates, and the household has to prove it" lands fast. |

**Verdict: too early to raise from funds. Raise from angels in 2 to 3 months, after launch.**

The reason is not the product. It is that the round has no evidence to price, and the business does not yet need the money: hosting is about R1,000 a month (estimate, `founder/pricing-strategy.md`) and break-even on fixed costs is 27 Shield subscribers. An investor will ask "why do you need R1m before your first customer?" and the honest answer today is "to pay myself". That is a fair use, but it needs data behind it.

**What to do first (8 weeks, starting at launch):**
1. Launch in Johannesburg and eThekwini through 5 residents' associations (`founder/pitch-deck.md`, slide 9).
2. Hit these numbers: 500 free accounts, 40 paying customers, 10 dispute letters sent, 3 disputes resolved with the rand credited recorded, and a measured CAC on at least one channel.
3. Collect 3 quotes from paying users and one before-and-after bill.
4. Then raise. Those numbers turn the Traction score from 1 to 3 and give the round a price.

## 2. Round size and terms

- **Amount: R1m (about $55,000).** That is right for this stage. Equidam's platform data puts the average African pre-seed capital need at $0.39m in H1 2026 ([Equidam H1 2026](https://www.equidam.com/startup-valuation-delta-h1-2026/)), but those are mostly teams, not solo founders before launch. R1m buys 12 months and the milestones in the deck. Do not stretch to R3m to "be safe": it raises the price you have to justify and the bar for the next round.
- **Instrument:** a convertible loan agreement under South African law, the common pre-seed form here, rather than a US SAFE.
  - Valuation cap: R10m post-money.
  - Discount: 20% to the next priced round.
  - Interest: 0% to 8%, converting rather than paid in cash.
  - Maturity: 24 months, converting at the cap if no round happens.
  - Have a South African startup lawyer draft one standard agreement for every investor. Foreign investors bring exchange-control paperwork, so check with the lawyer before taking offshore money.
- **Valuation range:** the only regional data found is Equidam's platform data, which reflects valuations founders ran on Equidam, not prices investors paid. Africa averaged $2.99m in H1 2026 ([Equidam H1 2026](https://www.equidam.com/startup-valuation-delta-h1-2026/)); the Q1 2026 median was $4.03m ([Equidam Q1 2026](https://www.equidam.com/startup-valuation-delta-q1-2026/)). No South Africa-specific pre-seed valuation data was found. **For a pre-launch solo founder, expect a cap of R8m to R12m** (about $0.45m to $0.65m). That is well below those averages, because you have no traction and no co-founder. With the 8-week numbers above, argue for the top of that range.
- **Runway:** 12 months at the use of funds in the deck (40% founder salary, 30% acquisition, 15% part-time support, 15% hosting, AI and legal).
- **Dilution:** R1m at a R10m post-money cap is **10%**. Equidam's African average dilution is 11.64% ([Equidam H1 2026](https://www.equidam.com/startup-valuation-delta-h1-2026/)). Do not go above 15% at this stage.

**The question every investor will ask: can this be big?** Prepare a two-sentence answer about the path beyond households: managing agents and body corporates (the Landlord plan is the start), then metros in other countries with the same estimated-billing problem. Without it, funds will pass, and only angels who like a profitable small business will invest.

## 3. Investor targeting

**Investor profile**
- **Stage:** angels and pre-seed programmes. South African funds with new money mostly write seed cheques: Grindstone's new R500m fund targets seed to Series A ([Disrupt Africa, Sept 2026](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/09/11/sas-grindstone-ventures-launches-new-31m-fund/)).
- **Cheque size:** R50,000 to R250,000 per angel; 5 to 10 angels fill the round.
- **Sector:** consumer fintech, proptech, civic tech. Best of all: people who own or manage rental property, because they pay these bills.
- **Geography:** Johannesburg, Durban and Cape Town.
- **Hands-on or passive:** hands-on for 1 or 2 of them: one with consumer marketing experience and one with property or municipal-law contacts.

**Target list framework (50 minimum)**
- **Tier 1, best fit (10):** South African angels who are property investors, managing agents or proptech founders, and who have written a pre-seed cheque in the last 18 months. They feel the problem personally and can introduce Landlord customers.
- **Tier 2, strong fit (20):** South African angels and angel networks with consumer fintech or proptech investments, plus pre-seed programmes that invest (Grindstone's accelerator route, see below).
- **Tier 3, backup (20):** non-dilutive and slower money: TIA seed funding, Orange Corners (founders aged 35 or under), develoPPP Ventures once you have revenue, and diaspora South African angels.

**Examples that could be linked to a recent early-stage South African deal.** Only two were found that meet the rule of naming no investor without a linked deal:
1. **Grindstone Ventures:** has invested at pre-seed and seed in companies from its programme, including Locstat, Welo and AgriLogiQ ([Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/09/11/sas-grindstone-ventures-launches-new-31m-fund/)). Apply to the programme rather than the fund.
2. **Oakvale Invest:** led a $61K pre-seed round for Tennsa in August 2026 ([AU-Startups tracker](https://au-startups.com/funding/countries/south-africa)), which is the right cheque size for this round.

The other 48 names come from the databases below. Check each one for a cheque in the last 18 months before adding them.

**Where to find investors**
- [Shizune's South African proptech angel list](https://shizune.co/investors/proptech-angel-investors-south-africa) and [proptech investor list](https://shizune.co/investors/proptech-investors-south-africa).
- [Ellty's Johannesburg seed investor list](https://www.ellty.com/investors/johannesburg-seed-investors), for 54 Collective and E4E Africa among others (third-party data; verify).
- [Launch Base Africa's deal tracker](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/10/26/african-startup-deal/): read every South African pre-seed deal and note who invested.
- OpenVC and Signal by NFX for angels who list South Africa.

**Warm intros, specifically:**
- Ask each residents' association chair you present to: "Who in this suburb invests in startups?" Chairs are often property professionals and know the local angels.
- Email the founders of the South African pre-seed companies on the trackers above. Ask for one intro to their angel, with a forwardable 4-line email attached.
- Ask your Supabase, PayFast or accelerator contacts for founders who raised under R2m in the last year; those founders know who writes small cheques.
- Never cold-email a fund partner before you have the 8-week numbers. You get one first impression.

## 5. Timeline

Adjusted for pre-launch: weeks 1 to 8 build the evidence, then the standard 12-week raise.

- **Weeks 1 to 8:** launch, run 5 association sessions, reach the numbers in section 1. Build the target list in parallel.
- **Weeks 9 to 10:** one-pager, financial model, data room, lawyer drafts the convertible loan agreement; send 20 warm-intro requests.
- **Weeks 11 to 12:** Tier 3 meetings and grant applications first, as practice.
- **Weeks 13 to 14:** Tier 2, with the questions you could not answer in Tier 3 fixed.
- **Weeks 15 to 16:** Tier 1, citing who has already committed.
- **Weeks 17 to 18:** follow-ups and diligence (data room, a call with a paying user).
- **Weeks 19 to 20:** sign the convertible loan agreements and close. Close on a rolling basis: take each signed cheque as it comes rather than waiting for the full R1m.

## 6. Mistakes to avoid

1. **Raising before launch.**
   - Why founders do it: it feels safer to have money first.
   - Instead: launch, because 8 weeks of data is worth more than a higher valuation now.
2. **Pitching venture funds a business that is too small for them.**
   - Why founders do it: funds are easier to find than angels.
   - Instead: target angels, and pitch funds only once the managing-agent and body-corporate path has paying customers.
3. **Letting the first investor set non-standard terms.**
   - Why founders do it: relief at the first yes.
   - Instead: one lawyer-drafted agreement, the same cap and discount for everyone.
4. **No co-founder or senior help story.**
   - Why founders do it: you built it alone, so it seems unnecessary.
   - Instead: name the part-time community and support lead and one advisor with municipal-billing or legal experience before the first meeting.
5. **Counting money recovered that is not yet credited.**
   - Why founders do it: overcharge estimates look like traction.
   - Instead: report only disputes resolved with the rand credited recorded, because investors will ask for proof.
